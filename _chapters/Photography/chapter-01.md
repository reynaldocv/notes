# Photography
This is a tutorial on photography. Photography is the art, application, and practice of creating durable images by recording light or other electromagnetic radiation, either electronically by means of an image sensor, or chemically by means of a light-sensitive material such as photographic film.

This tutorial is done on my first camera, a Canon EOS R7. And, this tutorial is based on my personal experience and knowledge. I will try to cover the basics of photography, and some advanced topics as well. I will also try to cover some of the common mistakes that beginners make (I am the beginner), and how to avoid them (if I can avoid it).

First, I will cover all mode in the camera Canon R7.

## Basic concepts in photography:
1. **Exposure:** The amount of light that reaches the camera sensor or film. It is determined by three factors: aperture, shutter speed, and ISO. A proper exposure is achieved when the right amount of light is captured to create a well-lit image. Overexposure results in a bright image with loss of detail, while underexposure results in a dark image with loss of detail.

2. **Aperture:** The opening in the lens through which light passes to enter the camera. It is measured in f-stops (e.g., f/2.8, f/4, f/5.6). A larger aperture (smaller f-stop number) allows more light to enter, resulting in a brighter image and a shallower depth of field (blurry background). A smaller aperture (larger f-stop number) allows less light to enter, resulting in a darker image and a greater depth of field (more of the scene in focus).

3. **Shutter Speed:** The amount of time the camera's shutter remains open to allow light to reach the sensor. It is measured in seconds or fractions of a second (e.g., 1/1000s, 1/60s, 2s). A faster shutter speed freezes motion, while a slower shutter speed can create motion blur. Shutter speed also affects exposure, with longer exposures allowing more light to reach the sensor.

4. **ISO:** The sensitivity of the camera's sensor to light. It is measured in numbers (e.g., ISO 100, ISO 400, ISO 1600). A lower ISO value results in less sensitivity to light and less noise (grain) in the image, while a higher ISO value increases sensitivity to light but also increases noise. It is important to balance ISO with aperture and shutter speed to achieve the desired exposure and image quality.

5. **White Balance:** The adjustment of colors in an image to make them appear natural under different lighting conditions. Different light sources (e.g., sunlight, incandescent bulbs, fluorescent lights) emit light with different color temperatures, which can cause images to have a color cast (e.g., too warm or too cool). White balance can be set manually or automatically, and it is important to choose the correct white balance setting to ensure accurate color representation in your photos.

----------------------------

6. Composition: The arrangement of elements within the frame of an image. Good composition helps to create visually appealing and engaging photographs. Common composition techniques include the rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, symmetry, and balance. Understanding and applying these techniques can help photographers create more compelling images.
7. Focus: The process of adjusting the camera's lens to make the subject of the photograph appear sharp and clear. Focus can be achieved manually or automatically, and it is important to ensure that the subject is in focus to create a visually appealing image. Depth of field, which is influenced by aperture, also affects how much of the image appears in focus.
8. Depth of Field: The distance between the nearest and farthest objects in a scene that appear acceptably sharp in an image. Depth of field is influenced by aperture, focal length, and distance to the subject. A shallow depth of field (achieved with a larger aperture) results in a blurred background, while a greater depth of field (achieved with a smaller aperture) keeps more of the scene in focus. Understanding depth of field is important for controlling the visual impact of your photographs.
9. Lighting: The quality, direction, and intensity of light in a scene. Good lighting is essential for creating visually appealing photographs. Natural light (e.g., sunlight) and artificial light (e.g., studio lights, flash) can be used to achieve different effects. Understanding how to work with light, including its color temperature and direction, can greatly enhance the quality of your images.


## Modes in Canon R7: 
We have 3 categories of modes in Canon R7, which are:

1. Basic Zone: Thinking of point and shoot, where the camera takes care of most of the settings automatically:

- A+ (Scene Intelligent Auto): Full automatic control of the camera.
- Special Scene Mode: Includes options like Portrait, Group Photo, Landscape, Panoramic, Sports, Kids, Panning, Close-up, Food, Night Portrait, Handheld Night Scene, and HDR Backlight Control.
- Creative Filters Mode: Applies various artistic effects to images.

2. Creative Zone: Offers advanced manual or semi-automatic control over exposure: las imágenes.

- Fv (Flexible-Priority AE): Allows switching between automatic or manual settings for shutter speed, aperture, ISO, and exposure compensation on the fly.
- P (Program AE): The camera selects the shutter speed and aperture, while you control ISO and other parameters.
- Tv (Shutter-Priority AE): Shutter speed priority; you choose the seconds/fractions of a second, and the camera calculates the aperture.
- Av (Aperture-Priority AE): Aperture priority; you choose the f-number, and the camera calculates the shutter speed.
- M (Manual Exposure): Full manual control of shutter speed, aperture, and ISO.
- B (Long / Bulb Exposures): Long exposure while the shutter button is held

3. Custom Modes (C1, C2, C3): Slots on the dial to save and recall sets of pre-configured settings (ideal for quickly switching between action, portrait, or video).
